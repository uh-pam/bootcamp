Trees: multiply, then add

★ essentialSM

Multiply both sides of the definition by P(B)P(B)P(B) to get the multiplication rule:

P(A∩B)=P(B) P(A∣B)P(A \cap B) = P(B)\,P(A \mid B)P(A∩B)=P(B)P(A∣B)

A probability tree draws it in two stages: first the season (a quarter of the months each), then wet or not, given the season. Multiply along a path; add the paths that end in the same outcome.

The seasons split the months with no overlap, so adding the wet paths gives the law of total probability:

P(wet)=∑iP(Bi) P(wet∣Bi)=14×52+32+43+5290=179360≈0.497P(\text{wet}) = \sum_i P(B_i)\,P(\text{wet} \mid B_i) = \tfrac{1}{4} \times \tfrac{52 + 32 + 43 + 52}{90} = \tfrac{179}{360} \approx 0.497P(wet)=i∑​P(Bi​)P(wet∣Bi​)=41​×9052+32+43+52​=360179​≈0.497

Independent events

★ essentialMS

The tree shows the season matters: P(wet∣Autumn)≈0.578P(\text{wet} \mid \text{Autumn}) \approx 0.578P(wet∣Autumn)≈0.578 but P(wet)≈0.497P(\text{wet}) \approx 0.497P(wet)≈0.497. Events are independent when learning one does not change the probability of the other:

P(A∣B)=P(A)equivalentlyP(A∩B)=P(A) P(B)P(A \mid B) = P(A) \qquad \text{equivalently} \qquad P(A \cap B) = P(A)\,P(B)P(A∣B)=P(A)equivalentlyP(A∩B)=P(A)P(B)

The second form is the multiplication rule with P(A∣B)P(A \mid B)P(A∣B) replaced by P(A)P(A)P(A): the “multiply for independent trials” rule of Lesson 8.2. To check a table, compare a row of normalize="index" with the overall share:
