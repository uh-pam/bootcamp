Idea · 7 min

Secant and tangent

★ essentialMP

In Lesson 1.3 the slope of a straight line was rise ÷ run. A curve has no single slope, but any two points on it do. The line through them is a secant, and its slope is the average rate of change:

ΔyΔx=f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}ΔxΔy​=b−af(b)−f(a)​

Δ\DeltaΔ (“delta”) means “change in”. The units are units of yyy per unit of xxx: °C per month, £ per year. With data, np.diff(y) / np.diff(x) gives the slope of every secant between neighbouring points; when the steps in xxx are not all 1, the division matters.

The tangent at a point touches the curve there and runs in the same direction. Its slope is the rate of change at that one point: its sign gives the direction and its size the steepness.

Worked example · 7 min

Shrink the gap

★ essentialMP

To get the tangent’s slope, slide the second point towards the first. Take b=a+hb = a + hb=a+h with a small step hhh: the secant turns towards the tangent.

Worked example: the slope of x² at x = 1
Python

With h=1h = 1h=1 the slope is 3, far from the truth: a big hhh gives a poor approximation. As hhh shrinks, the slopes settle on 2, the slope of the tangent. That number is the derivative of fff at 1, written f′(1)f'(1)f′(1). From the code:

f′(a)=lim⁡h→0f(a+h)−f(a)hf'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}f′(a)=h→0lim​hf(a+h)−f(a)​

lim⁡h→0\lim_{h \to 0}limh→0​ reads “the value this approaches as hhh shrinks to 0”. Another name for f′(x)f'(x)f′(x) is dydx\dfrac{dy}{dx}dxdy​: a tiny change in yyy over a tiny change in xxx. A computer cannot reach h=0h = 0h=0, but h=0.00001h = 0.00001h=0.00001 is close enough: that gives a numerical derivative.
