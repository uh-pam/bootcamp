Use it · 7 min

Two inputs: slopes on a map

★ essentialMSP

Most models have several inputs, such as the slope and intercept of a line. A function f(x,y)f(x, y)f(x,y) gives a height at every point of the plane: a landscape. A contour plot maps it; each line joins points of equal height.

Python

A bowl, lowest at (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0). On a hillside the slope depends on which way you walk, so there is one derivative for each input. The partial derivative ∂f∂x\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}∂x∂f​ is the slope when only xxx moves: differentiate as usual, treating yyy as a fixed number. For f(x,y)=x2+3y2f(x, y) = x^2 + 3y^2f(x,y)=x2+3y2:

∂f∂x=2x+0=2x,∂f∂y=0+6y=6y\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2x + 0 = 2x, \qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 0 + 6y = 6y∂x∂f​=2x+0=2x,∂y∂f​=0+6y=6y

When only xxx moves, 3y23y^23y2 is a constant, so its derivative is 0. Put the two together and you get the gradient, a vector:

∇f=(∂f∂x,∂f∂y)=(2x,  6y)\nabla f = \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right) = (2x,\; 6y)∇f=(∂x∂f​,∂y∂f​)=(2x,6y)

At (1,1)(1, 1)(1,1), ∇f=(2,6)\nabla f = (2, 6)∇f=(2,6): the arrow on the map. The gradient points straight uphill, across the contour line at right angles, and is longer where the slope is steeper. Downhill is −∇f-\nabla f−∇f; at the bottom, ∇f=(0,0)\nabla f = (0, 0)∇f=(0,0).
