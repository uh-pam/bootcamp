Worked example · 6 min

From covariance to r

★ essentialMSP

Covariance has a flaw: its units. For flipper length and body mass, sxy=9852s_{xy} = 9852sxy​=9852 mm·g; weigh the same penguins in kg and it becomes 9.85 mm·kg. Divide by both SDs and the units cancel:

r=sxysx syr = \frac{s_{xy}}{s_x\, s_y}r=sx​sy​sxy​​

This is Pearson’s correlation coefficient. It has no units and always lies between −1 and 1. Its sign gives the direction; its size, the strength of the straight-line pattern.

Use it · 4 min

Correlation is not causation

★ essentialSP

Back to the bills, now split by species.

Python

Within every species, longer bills go with deeper ones: rrr = 0.39, 0.65 and 0.65. Mixed together, r=−0.23r = -0.23r=−0.23, because Gentoos have long but shallow bills. A trend that reverses when you split the groups is Simpson’s paradox. Species is a confounder: a third variable linked to both columns.

A correlation, however strong, can come from:

SourceExample
a confounderice-cream sales and sunburn cases both rise with sunshine
reverse directionareas with more crime get more police patrols; the patrols don’t cause the crime
coincidencetwo unrelated series that both happen to rise over the same years

A correlation alone doesn’t show cause. A randomised experiment, where chance decides who gets xxx, gives much stronger grounds: random assignment balances the confounders on average.

Right. rrr describes an association. A likely confounder is population: bigger towns have more of both.

A correlation cannot show a cause. Bigger towns have more cafés and more burglaries: population is a confounder.
Closing cafés would reduce burglaries
That is a causal claim about an intervention, which only an experiment could support. Both counts probably grow with population.
r=0.7r = 0.7r=0.7 is too weak to mean anything
0.7 is a fairly strong straight-line association. The limit is not its size but what it can show: association, not cause.
