Reading slope, intercept and r²

★ essentialMS

Back to all 333 penguins, y^=50.15x−5872.1\hat{y} = 50.15x - 5872.1y^​=50.15x−5872.1.

NumberWhat it meansPenguins
Slope mmmchange in predicted yyy for each 1-unit increase in xxx, in units of yyy per unit of xxxpenguins with 1 mm longer flippers are about 50 g heavier, on average
Intercept cccpredicted yyy at x=0x = 0x=0−5872 g at 0 mm: an impossible mass
r2r^2r2the proportion of the variation in yyy that the line accounts for0.762

The slope describes an association across penguins, not what would happen if one penguin’s flippers grew (Lesson 6.2: correlation is not causation). The intercept is often an extrapolation: the flippers in the data run from 172 to 231 mm, far from 0. It anchors the line; read it as a real value only when x=0x = 0x=0 lies inside the data.

r². Compare what the line leaves over with the variation around the mean, the best single number (Lesson 4.4):

r2=1−∑ei2∑(yi−yˉ)2r^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum e_i^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}r2=1−∑(yi​−yˉ​)2∑ei2​​

For a straight line with an intercept, this equals the square of Pearson’s rrr.

Python

Both give 0.762: about 76% of the variation in body mass is accounted for by flipper length. The other 24% is scatter around the line.
