Worked example · 6 min

The mean: add up, divide by n

★ essentialSMP

The mean of nnn values is their sum divided by nnn. We write it xˉ\bar{x}xˉ (“x-bar”):

xˉ=1n∑i=1nxi\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_ixˉ=n1​i=1∑n​xi​

Picture the values as weights on a see-saw: the mean is where it balances. For masses 3.2,3.6,3.7,4.0,5.53.2, 3.6, 3.7, 4.0, 5.53.2,3.6,3.7,4.0,5.5 kg the mean is 4.0 kg; the distances to its left add up to 0.8+0.4+0.3=1.50.8 + 0.4 + 0.3 = 1.50.8+0.4+0.3=1.5, and the one to its right is also 1.51.51.5.

Because 1n\tfrac{1}{n}n1​ multiplies every value, the mean is also a dot product (Lesson 2.1):

xˉ=(1n,1n,…,1n)⋅x\bar{x} = \left(\tfrac{1}{n}, \tfrac{1}{n}, \dots, \tfrac{1}{n}\right) \cdot \mathbf{x}xˉ=(n1​,n1​,…,n1​)⋅x

Use it · 6 min

The median, and the typo

★ essentialSP

The median is the middle value once the data are sorted:

  • odd nnn: take the middle value. For (3.2,3.6,3.7,4.0,5.5)(3.2, 3.6, \mathbf{3.7}, 4.0, 5.5)(3.2,3.6,3.7,4.0,5.5) the median is 3.7.
  • even nnn: take the mean of the two middle values.

In code: np.median(x) or df["body_mass_g"].median(). Now suppose one mass, 4500 g in row 12, was typed as 45000 g (the hook’s question):

Python

The mean jumps by 122 g: the extra 40,500 g is shared among 333 penguins. The median does not move, because it only uses the order: row 12 was above the middle before, and it still is.

The mean uses every value, which is its strength and its weakness:

DataBetter “typical” valueWhy
Household incomes, house pricesmediana few huge values pull the mean up
Reaction timesmediana few very slow responses
Body mass of one penguin speciesmean (or either)roughly symmetric, no extreme values: the two agree
