The diagnostic

Fifteen questions, about twenty minutes. Its only purpose is to tell you which lessons you can skip.

It is not a test, nothing is recorded, and there is no pass mark. Answer honestly — the cost of pretending you can do something is that you skip a lesson you needed.

How to take it. Work on paper, without a calculator except where one is obviously needed, and without running any code. Write your answer down before opening the solution: an answer you nearly had is not the same as one you had, and only you can tell the difference. Keep a note of which questions you missed; the routing table at the end turns that list into a reading plan.

Python

1. What does this print?

print(3 / 2, 3 // 2, 7 % 3)
TipShow the answer

1.5 1 1

/ always gives a float (a number with a decimal part), // divides and rounds down to a whole number, and % gives the remainder. If you expected 1 from the first one, you are thinking of Python 2 or a language like it, where dividing two whole numbers drops the remainder.

Covers P2.

2. What does this print?

marks = [41, 58, 67, 72, 90]
print(marks[1:3], marks[-1])
TipShow the answer

[58, 67] 90

Positions (indices) count from 0, so marks[1] is 58. A slice a:b starts at position a and stops before b, so it has \(b - a\) elements. Negative indices count back from the end, so marks[-1] is the last element.

Covers P3.

3. What array does this produce, and what shape is it?

import numpy as np
grid = np.arange(6).reshape(2, 3)
print(grid + np.array([10, 20, 30]))
TipShow the answer 
[[10 21 32]
 [13 24 35]]

Shape \((2, 3)\). The one-dimensional array of length 3 is broadcast down both rows. If you expected an error, or expected the addition to go the other way, read the NumPy lessons.

Covers P6 and P7.

4. A pandas DataFrame df has a column score containing some missing values. What does df[df["score"] > 70]["score"].mean() compute, and what happens to the missing values?

TipShow the answer

It keeps the rows whose score exceeds 70 and averages that column. Rows with a missing score are silently dropped by the comparison — pandas stores a missing value as NaN, and NaN > 70 is False — and .mean() would skip any that survived anyway.

The important half of the answer is “silently”. Nothing warns you, and the count you would have reported is smaller than you think.

Covers P8 and P9.

Mathematics

5. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4} (2i - 1)\).

TipShow the answer

\(1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16\).

The notation says “put \(i = 1, 2, 3, 4\) into \(2i - 1\) in turn, and add the results”. If reading it aloud was the hard part rather than the arithmetic, M1 is written for you.

Covers M1.

6. Solve \(2^x = 32\). Then write \(\ln(e^3)\) and \(\log(ab)\) more simply.

TipShow the answer

\(x = 5\), since \(2^5 = 32\). \(\ln(e^3) = 3\), because a logarithm undoes the exponential. \(\log(ab) = \log a + \log b\) — logs turn multiplication into addition, which is the whole reason they exist.

Covers M2.

7. A straight line passes through \((2, 5)\) and \((6, 17)\). What is its gradient, and what does a gradient of 3 mean in words?

TipShow the answer

\((17 - 5)/(6 - 2) = 12/4 = 3\).

In words: for every 1 the input increases, the output increases by 3. If the arithmetic was fine but the sentence was not, do not skip M3 — the sentence is the part everything later depends on.

Covers M3.

8. Differentiate \(f(x) = x^3 - 4x + 1\).

TipShow the answer

\(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 4\).

Bring the power down and reduce it by one; the derivative of a constant is zero.

Covers M4.

9. Where does \(g(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5\) take its smallest value, and what is that value?

TipShow the answer

\(g'(x) = 2x - 6\), which is zero at \(x = 3\). There \(g(3) = 9 - 18 + 5 = -4\).

It is a minimum rather than a maximum because the parabola opens upwards — a check worth making rather than assuming.

Covers M5.

10. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^2 3x^2\,dx\).

TipShow the answer

\(\bigl[x^3\bigr]_0^2 = 8 - 0 = 8\).

If you also said “that is the area under the curve \(y = 3x^2\) between 0 and 2”, you have the reading that S5 needs.

Covers M7.

11. The matrix \(A\) is \(3 \times 4\) and the matrix \(B\) is \(4 \times 2\). What is the shape of \(AB\)? What about \(BA\)?

TipShow the answer

\(AB\) is \(3 \times 2\): the inner dimensions (4 and 4) must match and they cancel, leaving the outer ones. \(BA\) does not exist — \(B\) has 2 columns and \(A\) has 3 rows, and \(2 \ne 3\).

Shape arithmetic is most of what goes wrong in practice, and it is checkable before you run anything.

Covers M8 and M9.

12. Solve \(2x + y = 7\) and \(x - y = 2\).

TipShow the answer

Add the two equations: \(3x = 9\), so \(x = 3\), and then \(y = 1\).

Check both: \(2(3) + 1 = 7\) ✓ and \(3 - 1 = 2\) ✓. Substituting your answer back is a five-second habit that catches most arithmetic slips.

Covers M10.

Statistics

13. For the values \(4, 4, 5, 9, 13\): give the mean, the median, and say which of the two is larger and why.

TipShow the answer

Mean \(= 35/5 = 7\). Median \(= 5\) (the middle value once sorted).

The mean is larger because the two big values pull it up, while the median notices only that they are above the middle. (For completeness, the sample standard deviation is 3.94, dividing by \(n - 1 = 4\).)

Covers S2.

14. Of 200 students, 80 use the library weekly and 60 of those 80 passed. Of the 120 who do not, 60 passed. What is the probability that a student passed given that they use the library? And that they use the library given that they passed?

TipShow the answer

\(P(\text{pass} \mid \text{library}) = 60/80 = 0.75\).

For the other one, count all the passers: \(60 + 60 = 120\). So \(P(\text{library} \mid \text{pass}) = 60/120 = 0.50\).

Two different numbers from the same 60 students, because the denominator changed. If those came out equal, S4 is the most valuable lesson on this site for you.

Covers S4.

15. A sample of 64 measurements has a standard deviation of 16.

  1. What is the standard error of the mean?
  2. The resulting 95% confidence interval is \((8.1, 15.9)\). Is it correct to say “there is a 95% probability the population mean lies between 8.1 and 15.9”?
TipShow the answer

  1. \(16/\sqrt{64} = 16/8 = 2\).

  2. No. The 95% describes the procedure: across many repeated studies, 95% of the intervals built this way contain the true mean. This particular interval either contains it or does not.

If (a) was fine and (b) was not, read S8 — the distinction it draws is the one most often got wrong in published work.

Covers S7 and S8.

What to do with your answers

Find each question you missed and read the lessons in its row. If you answered a row’s questions confidently, skip or skim those lessons — but do glance at each lesson’s Before you start box before moving past it, since that is where a lesson declares what it assumes.

Three things the table cannot tell you.

A confident wrong answer is worse than a blank. If you were sure and wrong, read the lesson rather than skimming it: what needs replacing is a belief, not a gap.

No question tests S1 or S12, because no short question can. They are about choosing what to ask and recognising how an answer goes wrong, and they are the two lessons most likely to change how you work. Read both regardless of how you scored.

Seven more lessons have no question of their own: P5, P10, P11, M12, S6, S10 and S11. Read each one unless you can answer its Check yourself questions without looking.

If you missed most of a track’s questions, ignore the table for that track and read it in order from its first lesson. The table is for skipping what you know, not for choosing a path through what you do not.

Nobody skips everything. If you answered all fifteen comfortably, you do not need the mechanics — but the Common wrong turns block in each lesson is worth twenty minutes on its own, and it is where most of the material that is new to experienced readers lives.

Then go back to the units. Take the lessons you still need in the order the units give them, starting from Unit 1 and passing over the ones you can skip. The units keep each lesson after everything it relies on, which a list of lessons to read does not.
