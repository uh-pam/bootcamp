Choose8c

stats.norm.pdf(217.2, 217.2, 6.6) returns 0.060. What does 0.060 tell you?

The height of the PDF at 217.2 mm, a density per mm; only areas are probabilities
Right: a height times a width gives an area. The probability of a band 1 mm wide around 217.2 is about 0.060×1=0.060.060 \times 1 = 0.060.060×1=0.06.
6% of Gentoos have flippers of exactly 217.2 mm
That reads the height as a probability. For a continuous model P(X=217.2)=0P(X = 217.2) = 0P(X=217.2)=0: a single value has no area.
6% of Gentoos have flippers up to 217.2 mm
That would be the CDF, and at the mean it is 0.5: half the area lies left of μ\muμ.

Use it · 5 min

Quantiles: the CDF backwards

★ essentialMSP

The CDF turns a length into a share. A quantile goes back: the value qqq with F(q)=pF(q) = pF(q)=p. In SciPy that is stats.norm.ppf(p, mu, sigma), the inverse of cdf.

CallTakesReturns
stats.norm.cdf(x, mu, sigma)a value xxxa share F(x)F(x)F(x)
stats.norm.ppf(p, mu, sigma)a share ppp, between 0 and 1a value qqq

The middle 95% is μ±1.96σ\mu \pm 1.96\sigmaμ±1.96σ: the exact version of the rule’s 2 SDs. You will meet 1.96 again in Unit 10.

Choose8c

Which line gives the flipper length that 90% of Gentoos are below?

stats.norm.ppf(0.9, mu, sigma)
Right: ppf takes a share and returns the value qqq with F(q)=0.9F(q) = 0.9F(q)=0.9.
stats.norm.cdf(0.9, mu, sigma)
cdf takes a length, not a share: this is the share of flippers shorter than 0.9 mm, which is 0.
stats.norm.ppf(90, mu, sigma)
ppp is a proportion between 0 and 1: 0.9, not 90. With 90, SciPy returns nan.
stats.norm.ppf(0.1, mu, sigma)
That is the length 10% are below. “90% below” means F(q)=0.9F(q) = 0.9F(q)=0.9.
