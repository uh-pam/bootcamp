Idea · 7 min

Count the orders

★ essentialMSP

A random variable XXX is a number that comes out of a chance process; here, the number of right answers. XXX is binomial, X∼Binomial(n,p)X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, p)X∼Binomial(n,p), when:

  • there are nnn trials, fixed in advance;
  • each has two outcomes, success or failure;
  • each succeeds with the same probability ppp;
  • the trials are independent: one outcome doesn’t change the chances of the next (Unit 9 makes this precise).

For independent trials you multiply probabilities. Then XXX counts the successes, and P(X=k)P(X = k)P(X=k) needs two things: how many orders give kkk successes, and the probability of one order.

Two successes in four trials, three ways
S Picture
M Symbols

The number of orders is ”nnn choose kkk”:

(nk)=n!k! (n−k)!,(42)=4!2! 2!=244=6\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}, \qquad \binom{4}{2} = \frac{4!}{2!\,2!} = \frac{24}{4} = 6(kn​)=k!(n−k)!n!​,(24​)=2!2!4!​=424​=6

Each order has kkk factors ppp and n−kn - kn−k factors 1−p1 - p1−p. Add up the (nk)\binom{n}{k}(kn​) equal orders:

P(X=k)=(nk) pk (1−p)n−kP(X = k) = \binom{n}{k}\, p^k\, (1 - p)^{n-k}P(X=k)=(kn​)pk(1−p)n−k

The list of P(X=k)P(X = k)P(X=k) for every kkk is the probability mass function (pmf), hence pmf. Draw it for the guessing test:

At least, and a simulation check

★ essentialSP

stats.binom.cdf(k, n, p) gives P(X≤k)P(X \le k)P(X≤k), the sum of the pmf up to kkk. “At least 6” is the complement of “at most 5”:

P(X≥6)=1−P(X≤5)P(X \ge 6) = 1 - P(X \le 5)P(X≥6)=1−P(X≤5)

so it is 1 - stats.binom.cdf(5, 10, 0.25), not cdf(6, …): that would remove X=6X = 6X=6 too.

A simulation check tests your formula against chance. rng.binomial(n, p, size=10000) gives 10,000 values of XXX; the share of each kkk should be close to the pmf.

Python

Which count XXX is binomial?

A player takes 20 free throws; each goes in with probability 0.7, whatever happened on the others. XXX = number that go in
Right: n=20n = 20n=20 fixed, two outcomes, the same ppp, independent throws. X∼Binomial(20,0.7)X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(20, 0.7)X∼Binomial(20,0.7).
From a group of 5 women and 5 men, pick 3 different people at random. XXX = number of women
Each pick changes the group: after a woman, the next is a woman with probability 4/94/94/9, not 5/95/95/9. The trials are not independent.
Roll a die until the first 6. XXX = number of rolls

A binomial counts successes. A mass is not a count of two-outcome trials.
