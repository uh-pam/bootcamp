Idea · 6 min

Events are sets of outcomes

★ essentialMSP

Each possible result of a random pick is an outcome. The sample space SSS is the set of all outcomes: here the 333 penguins. An event is a subset of SSS, such as AAA = “a Gentoo”. When every outcome is equally likely:

P(A)=∣A∣∣S∣=119333≈0.357P(A) = \frac{|A|}{|S|} = \frac{119}{333} \approx 0.357P(A)=∣S∣∣A∣​=333119​≈0.357

It is also a long-run relative frequency: pick again and again, putting each penguin back, and the share of Gentoos settles near 0.357.

Use it · 5 min

Events that cannot overlap

★ essentialMS

Events with no outcome in common, A∩B=∅A \cap B = \emptysetA∩B=∅, are mutually exclusive (disjoint). Then P(A∩B)=0P(A \cap B) = 0P(A∩B)=0 and the rule loses its last term:

P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)when A∩B=∅P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) \qquad \text{when } A \cap B = \emptysetP(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)when A∩B=∅

Gentoo and Chinstrap are disjoint: a penguin has one species. So are Gentoo and Dream: no Gentoo in this data lives on Dream. So P(Gentoo∪Dream)=119+123333≈0.727P(\text{Gentoo} \cup \text{Dream}) = \tfrac{119 + 123}{333} \approx 0.727P(Gentoo∪Dream)=333119+123​≈0.727. Disjoint is not the same as independent, a different idea that comes in Unit 9.

The complement of “Gentoo or female” is “not Gentoo and not female” (De Morgan, Unit 1):

P(Ac∩Bc)=1−P(A∪B)P(A^c \cap B^c) = 1 - P(A \cup B)P(Ac∩Bc)=1−P(A∪B)

Wrap-up · 1 min

Wrap-up

You can now…

  • describe a sample space and its events as sets, and find P(A)=∣A∣/∣S∣P(A) = |A| / |S|P(A)=∣A∣/∣S∣ for equally likely outcomes;
  • use the complement rule and the addition rule, subtracting the overlap unless the events are disjoint;
  • find probabilities in pandas as the .mean() of a True/False mask.
