Idea · 6 min

Population, sample, statistic

★ essentialMSP

The population is everything you want to know about; a sample is the part you measure. A number that describes the population is a parameter. The same summary computed from a sample is a statistic.

Population and samples, three ways

SizeN=333N = 333N=333n=10n = 10n=10
Meanμ=4207.1\mu = 4207.1μ=4207.1 gxˉ\bar{x}xˉ

For a whole population, the SD divides by NNN: 804.0 g (805.2 g with N−1N - 1N−1).

P Code

A parameter is fixed. A statistic changes from sample to sample: that is sampling variability. It is not a mistake; it is what sampling does.

rng.choice(pop, size=n, replace=False) picks nnn different penguins, each equally likely. replace=False means no penguin is picked twice, as when you weigh real animals. The choice must be random: any rule of your own can favour one kind of penguin.

Right: it is computed from the sample, so another sample gives another value.

Right: it is computed from the sample too, and varies with it.
The mean mass of all 333 penguins, μ\muμ
That is a parameter. Every sample comes from the same 333 penguins, so μ\muμ stays 4207.1 g.
The size of the population, N=333N = 333N=333
That describes the population. It does not change when you draw a new sample.

Worked example · 7 min

Two thousand samples, one histogram

★ essentialSP

One sample mean says little about how much means vary. So repeat: draw many samples, keep each mean, and plot them all. The distribution of all those means is the sampling distribution of xˉ\bar{x}xˉ.

Worked example: the sampling distribution of mean body mass, n = 10
Python
  1. Centre: the means centre on 4204.0 g, close to μ=4207.1\mu = 4207.1μ=4207.1 g.

Use it · 6 min

Bigger samples, narrower spread

★ essentialSP

Now compare two sample sizes on the same axes, so the widths can be compared by eye.

Python

Both histograms centre near μ\muμ. With n=40n = 40n=40, the SD of the means is 118.4 g, against 249.7 g with n=10n = 10n=10. Bigger samples give a narrower sampling distribution. The population’s own spread, σ=804.0\sigma = 804.0σ=804.0 g, stays the same. Lesson 7.2 puts a formula on this.

Use it · 3 min

More practice

S

Optional: what does, and does not, make the means vary less.

Choose7a

A researcher wants sample means that vary less. A colleague says: “We need a bigger population.” What would help?

Bigger samples from the same population
Right. The spread of xˉ\bar{x}xˉ depends mainly on the sample size nnn, not on how big the population is.
A bigger population
A poll of 1000 people varies about as much in a country of 10 million as in one of 100 million. The size of each sample is what counts.
More samples of the same size
More samples give a smoother histogram of the same width. Each mean still varies as much as before.
Sampling with replacement
A survey sample of distinct penguins is drawn without replacement. With replacement a penguin can count twice, and the means vary a little more, not less.

Go deeper (optional) · Why a bell?

The central limit theorem says that for large enough nnn, the sampling distribution of xˉ\bar{x}xˉ is close to normal, whatever the shape of the population. Try the worked example with size=2 and with size=40 to watch the bell appear.

Choose7a

You want the mean hours of sleep of the 400 students on a course, from a sample of 40. Which method gives a random sample?

Number the students 0 to 399 and pick with rng.choice(400, size=40, replace=False)
Right: every student has the same chance, and nobody is picked twice.
Ask the first 40 students who arrive at a 9 am lecture
Early arrivers may well sleep differently from the rest. The method favours one kind of student, so it is biased.
Post an online survey and use the first 40 replies
People who choose to reply, and reply fast, may differ from the rest. A self-selected sample is biased.
Pick with rng.choice(400, size=40, replace=True)
With replacement, one student can be picked twice and counted twice. A survey of distinct students is drawn without replacement.
