Idea · 6 min

Resample your sample

★ essentialSP

A bootstrap resample draws nnn values from your sample with replacement: every draw can pick any value, even one already drawn. Some values appear twice, others not at all, so its mean xˉ∗\bar{x}^*xˉ∗ differs from xˉ\bar{x}xˉ: one mean you might have got.

One resample of five penguins
S Picture
12345mean
sample (g)350039003650352537253660
resample (g)372535253650390039003740

3900 appears twice and 3500 not at all, so the mean moves up by 80 g.

M Symbols

Draw x1∗,…,xn∗x^*_1, \dots, x^*_nx1∗​,…,xn∗​ with replacement from x1,…,xnx_1, \dots, x_nx1​,…,xn​, and take

xˉ∗=1n∑i=1nxi∗\bar{x}^* = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x^*_ixˉ∗=n1​i=1∑n​xi∗​

Repeat BBB times. The SD of the BBB values of xˉ∗\bar{x}^*xˉ∗ is the bootstrap SE, and it is close to s/ns/\sqrt{n}s/n​.

Now draw without replacement: rng.choice(x, size=len(x), replace=False) for the same five values. What is the mean of that resample, whatever the seed?

That is the resample with replacement above. Without replacement, every value is drawn exactly once.
Without replacement, five draws from five values use each value once, in some order.

Choose7b

x holds a sample of 40 values. Which line makes one bootstrap resample?

rng.choice(x, size=len(x), replace=True)
Right: 40 draws, each from all 40 values, so values can repeat.
rng.choice(x, size=len(x), replace=False)
That gives the same 40 values back in a new order, so every mean is xˉ\bar{x}xˉ.
rng.choice(x, size=20, replace=True)
A resample has the same size nnn as the sample. Means of 20 values vary more than means of 40, so the spread would overstate the SE.
rng.choice(x)
That draws a single value, not a resample of 40.

Worked example · 7 min

2000 resamples and an interval

★ essentialSP
Worked example: a bootstrap interval for the mean Chinstrap body mass
Python

The 2000 bootstrap means have SD 47.1 g, close to s/n=46.6s/\sqrt{n} = 46.6s/n​=46.6 g. Their middle 95%, from the 2.5th to the 97.5th percentile, is the 95% percentile interval: 3644 g to 3826 g (dashed lines).

Interpret: a range of plausible values for μ\muμ, the mean mass of all such Chinstraps, if the 68 are a random sample. It is close to xˉ±2 SE\bar{x} \pm 2\,\mathrm{SE}xˉ±2SE. It does not hold 95% of the penguins: their middle 95% runs from about 3100 g to 4500 g. For another statistic, such as the median, change resample.mean() to np.median(resample).

Use it · 6 min

Error bars say what they show

★ essentialSP

An error bar runs up and down from a group’s mean. Its length might be the SD, the SE or a 95% interval, and the same data give very different bars:

So always say what the bars show, in the title, legend or caption. For SE or 95% bars: if two bars overlap a lot, the difference could be sampling variation; if they are far apart, the means very likely differ. These are rules of thumb, not proof; Unit 10 makes them formal tests.

Choose7b

In panel A, the Adelie and Chinstrap bars overlap a lot. What does that tell you?

Many Adelies and Chinstraps have similar flippers; for the means, look at panel B
Right: SD bars show the spread of individuals. The SE bars in B are far apart (190.1 mm and 195.8 mm, each within 1 mm), so the means very likely differ.
The two means could be equal: the gap could be sampling variation
That reads SD bars as the uncertainty of the mean. SD bars stay long however many penguins you measure; SE bars shrink with nnn.
Nothing: error bars can’t be read without a formal test
Bars that say what they show can be read, with care. Formal tests (Unit 10) sharpen the rule of thumb.

In code, plt.errorbar(x, y, yerr=...) puts a dot at each y with a bar yerr up and down. Bars of ±2 SE\pm 2\,\mathrm{SE}±2SE are close to the 95% interval.

Wrap-up · 1 min

Wrap-up

You can now…

  • bootstrap: resample your sample with replacement, BBB times, and compute the statistic each time;
  • give a 95% percentile interval and read it as plausible values for the population mean, if the sample is random;
  • draw error bars, say what they show, and read overlap with care.
