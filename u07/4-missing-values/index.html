You bootstrap the mean of a sample of 50 values. What is one bootstrap resample?

50 values drawn from the sample with replacement
Right: some values appear twice and some not at all, so each resample’s mean differs a little. Repeat many times and take the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the means.
50 values drawn from the sample without replacement
That is the same 50 values in a new order, so every resample would have the same mean and the interval would have no width.
50 new values drawn from the population
The bootstrap has only the sample. Resampling it stands in for the new samples you cannot take.
25 values drawn from the sample with replacement
A resample has the same size as the sample, n=50n = 50n=50: the spread of the means depends on nnn, so a smaller resample gives too wide an interval.

Idea · 5 min

Finding the gaps

★ essentialSP

pandas marks a missing value as NaN, “not a number”. The file writes its gaps as NA; pd.read_csv reads those, and empty cells, as NaN. df.isna() turns the table into True/False, and sums count the Trues.

Gaps, three ways
S Picture
M Symbols

For one column of a table with nnn rows:

present+missing=nshare missing=missingn\text{present} + \text{missing} = n \qquad\qquad \text{share missing} = \frac{\text{missing}}{n}present+missing=nshare missing=nmissing​

For body_mass_g: 342+2=344342 + 2 = 344342+2=344, and 2/344=0.6%2/344 = 0.6\%2/344=0.6%. For sex: 11/344=3.2%11/344 = 3.2\%11/344=3.2%.

Drop only what you need

★ essentialP

df.dropna() removes every row with a gap in any column: whole rows, not cells. df.dropna(subset=["body_mass_g"]) looks only at the columns in the list. For a question about mass, the 9 rows missing only sex are still good data.

Fill, and say why

★ essentialSP

Filling (imputing) writes a value into each gap, usually the column’s median or mean. Flag the gaps first, then assign the result back to the column:

Not df["flipper_length_mm"].fillna(med, inplace=True): in pandas 3 that chained form leaves df unchanged.

Your code7c

Replace the two ___: store the median flipper length in med, then fill only the gaps in flipper_length_mm with it. Then press Run.

Filling keeps all nnn rows, but each filled value is invented, at the centre. It adds no spread, so the SD shrinks (here only from 14.06 to 14.02 mm; with many gaps, a lot), and links with other columns weaken.

Your answer7c

A survey asks 8 people their income, in £ thousands. Six answer: 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 60. The two who skip earn 80 and 110, but you cannot see that. You fill both gaps with the mean of the answers. What is the mean of the 8 values?

That is the true mean, with 80 and 110. A fill cannot recover values it never saw.
That fills the gaps with 0: 210/8210 / 8210/8. The mean of the answers is 210/6=35210 / 6 = 35210/6=35.
Solution
The answers’ mean is 210/6=35210 / 6 = 35210/6=35. With two 35s added: (210+70)/8=35(210 + 70) / 8 = 35(210+70)/8=35.

Dropping gives 35 too; the truth is 50. The high earners are missing because of their income, so both choices are biased, and filling makes it look precise: the SD falls from 14.1 to 12.0, against a true 31.3. Choose by how many values are missing, and why:

SituationWhat to doCost
a few gaps, plausibly at random (2 of 344 masses)drop the rows that miss a column you use: dropna(subset=[...])tiny: a small loss of nnn
many gapsno choice is free. Drop, or fill with the median and flag the filled rows; compare the answer with and without them (a sensitivity check)dropping raises the SE, and biases the result if the gaps aren’t random; filling invents values, shrinks the SD and weakens relationships
gaps related to the value itselfneither dropping nor filling removes the bias: say so, and look for more informationboth shift the mean
a text column such as sexdrop by subset when you use it; never fill it with a meana mean of text does not exist

Whatever you choose, write it down: what you did, and how many rows it touched.

In a wellbeing survey, 30% of people skipped the stress question, and the most stressed were the least likely to answer. Which statement is right?

Dropping and mean-filling both understate stress: report how many are missing and why
Right: the gaps are related to the value itself, so the answers that remain are the less stressed ones. Neither dropping nor filling removes that bias: say so in the write-up, and look for more information.
Mean-filling removes the bias, because it keeps every row
The fill repeats the mean of the answers, which is already too low. Keeping nnn does not bring back the stressed people.
Dropping removes the bias, because only real answers remain
The real answers come mostly from the less stressed people, so their mean is too low.
Fill the gaps with 0, so no one is left out
0 means “no stress”: it invents values and pulls the mean down even further.
