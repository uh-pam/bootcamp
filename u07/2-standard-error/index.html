Idea · 6 min

The standard error

★ essentialMSP

The SD of the sampling distribution of xˉ\bar{x}xˉ is called the standard error of the mean. It has a formula:

SE=σn\mathrm{SE} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}SE=n​σ​

Here σ\sigmaσ is the population SD. For all 333 penguins, np.std(pop) divides by NNN, because this is the whole population: σ=804.0\sigma = 804.0σ=804.0 g (805.2 g with n−1n - 1n−1).

The formula assumes independent draws: with replacement, as here, or from a population much larger than the sample. Lesson 7.1 drew distinct penguins without replacement; from only 333, that gives a smaller spread (see the Aside).

The SE shrinks with n\sqrt{n}n​, not with nnn:

σ4n=σ2n,σ100n=σ10n\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{4n}} = \frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt{n}}, \qquad \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{100n}} = \frac{\sigma}{10\sqrt{n}}4n​σ​=2n​σ​,100n​σ​=10n​σ​

To halve the SE you need 4 times the data; to divide it by 10, 100 times.

P Code
Go deeper (optional) · Why sample with replacement here?

Lesson 7.1 drew without repeats. A sample of 160 is almost half of our 333 penguins, and then the means vary less: about 46 g, not 63.6 g (for n=100n = 100n=100, 67 g, not 80.4 g). The factor is (N−n)/(N−1)\sqrt{(N - n)/(N - 1)}(N−n)/(N−1)​, the finite-population correction. It is close to 1 when nnn is a small part of NNN, as in most surveys. Drawing with replacement acts like an endless population, where σ/n\sigma/\sqrt{n}σ/n​ holds.

Use it · 6 min

SD or SE?

★ essentialSP

The SD and the SE answer different questions.

  • The SD, sss, is about individual penguins: how far a typical penguin is from the mean. It does not shrink as nnn grows.
  • The SE, s/ns/\sqrt{n}s/n​, is about the mean: how far xˉ\bar{x}xˉ typically is from μ\muμ. It shrinks with n\sqrt{n}n​.
Python

The SD stays near 800 g (790.6, 795.1, 800.3); the SE falls from 250.0 to 63.3 g. Bigger samples do not make penguins less varied; they pin down the mean.

The sample means in Lesson 7.1 made a bell-shaped histogram, so the 68–95–99.7 rule of Lesson 3.4 applies to them: about 95% of sample means lie within 2 SE of μ\muμ. Lesson 7.3 draws SE as error bars.
