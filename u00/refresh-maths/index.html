Negative numbers

M

Picture a number line. Adding moves right, subtracting moves left.

  • Subtracting a negative is adding: 5−(−3)=5+3=85 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 85−(−3)=5+3=8.
  • Multiplying or dividing: two numbers with the same sign give a positive answer; different signs give a negative one. So (−4)×(−3)=12(-4) \times (-3) = 12(−4)×(−3)=12 and (−12)÷3=−4(-12) \div 3 = -4(−12)÷3=−4.
  • Squares are never negative: (−3)2=(−3)×(−3)=9(-3)^2 = (-3) \times (-3) = 9(−3)2=(−3)×(−3)=9.

Rearranging a formula

M

To make a different letter the subject of a formula, undo what was done to it, in reverse order, and do the same thing to both sides.

Worked example: Celsius from Fahrenheit

The formula is F=1.8 C+32F = 1.8\,C + 32F=1.8C+32. We want CCC on its own.

  1. CCC was multiplied by 1.8, then 32 was added. Undo the last step first: subtract 32 from both sides.   F−32=1.8 C\;F - 32 = 1.8\,CF−32=1.8C
  2. Then undo the multiplication: divide both sides by 1.8.   F−321.8=C\;\dfrac{F - 32}{1.8} = C1.8F−32​=C
  3. Check with a value you know. Water boils at F=212F = 212F=212:   212−321.8=1801.8=100\;\dfrac{212 - 32}{1.8} = \dfrac{180}{1.8} = 1001.8212−32​=1.8180​=100. ✓

Every straight line (except a vertical one) has an equation

y=mx+cy = mx + cy=mx+c
  • mmm is the gradient: how much yyy goes up when xxx goes up by 1. Between two points it is change in ychange in x\dfrac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}change in xchange in y​. A positive mmm goes up to the right, a negative mmm goes down, and m=0m = 0m=0 is flat.
  • ccc is the intercept: the value of yyy where the line crosses the yyy-axis, at x=0x = 0x=0.

Sine and cosine

MP

ADS1 plots sine and cosine in week 1, and Unit 7 uses them for repeating patterns in data. Here is all you need to start.

Picture a circle of radius 1, centred at the origin. A point starts at (1,0)(1, 0)(1,0) and moves anticlockwise round the circle. When it has turned through an angle θ\thetaθ, its position is

(cos⁡θ, sin⁡θ)(\cos\theta,\ \sin\theta)(cosθ, sinθ)

So cos⁡θ\cos\thetacosθ is how far across the point is, and sin⁡θ\sin\thetasinθ is how far up.
