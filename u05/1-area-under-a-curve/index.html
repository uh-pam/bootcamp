Idea · 7 min

Area is an amount

★ essentialMSP

On a speed–time graph, a stretch at constant speed is a rectangle: height in km/h, width in hours. Its area, speed × time, is the distance in km.

The trip, three ways
S Picture
M Symbols

∫03v(t) dt=50×1+100×2=250\displaystyle\int_0^3 v(t)\,dt = 50 \times 1 + 100 \times 2 = 250∫03​v(t)dt=50×1+100×2=250 km

Units: km/h × h = km.

In general, the definite integral

∫abf(x) dx\int_a^b f(x)\,dx∫ab​f(x)dx

is the area between the curve y=f(x)y = f(x)y=f(x) and the xxx-axis, from x=ax = ax=a to x=bx = bx=b. The ∫\int∫ is a stretched S, for “sum”; dxdxdx is a very thin width. When fff is a rate, the integral accumulates it into a total, and the units multiply:

Rate on the yyy-axisxxx-axisArea
speed, km/hhoursdistance, km
power, kWhoursenergy, kWh
rainfall, mm/daydaysrainfall, mm

Area below the axis counts as negative: a negative rate takes away, like a tank draining. The integral is the net change.

Worked example · 8 min

Rectangles under a curve

★ essentialMP

For a curve, cut [a,b][a, b][a,b] into nnn strips of equal width Δx=b−an\Delta x = \dfrac{b - a}{n}Δx=nb−a​ and stand a rectangle on each. Here each height is the curve’s value at the strip’s left edge.

Worked example: the area under x² from 0 to 2, with 4 rectangles
  1. Width: Δx=2−04=0.5\Delta x = \dfrac{2 - 0}{4} = 0.5Δx=42−0​=0.5.
  2. Left edges: xi=0, 0.5, 1, 1.5x_i = 0,\ 0.5,\ 1,\ 1.5xi​=0, 0.5, 1, 1.5. Not 2: that is the right edge of the last strip.
  3. Heights: f(xi)=xi2=0, 0.25, 1, 2.25f(x_i) = x_i^2 = 0,\ 0.25,\ 1,\ 2.25f(xi​)=xi2​=0, 0.25, 1, 2.25.
  4. Add height × width: (0+0.25+1+2.25)×0.5=1.75(0 + 0.25 + 1 + 2.25) \times 0.5 = 1.75(0+0.25+1+2.25)×0.5=1.75.

Wrap-up · 1 min

Wrap-up

You can now…

  • read a definite integral as the signed area under a curve, and as a total built up from a rate, with units;
  • work out a rectangle sum by hand: width Δx\Delta xΔx, heights f(xi)f(x_i)f(xi​), add height × width;
  • approximate an integral in NumPy with np.linspace(a, b, n, endpoint=False) and np.sum(f(x) * dx).

Keep this pattern:
