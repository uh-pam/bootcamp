Idea · 7 min

Fitted values and residuals

★ essentialMS

The line gives each penguin a fitted value y^i\hat{y}_iy^​i​ (“y-hat”). The gap between what we observed and what the line says is the residual.

A residual, three ways
S Picture
M Symbols
y^i=mxi+cei=yi−y^i(observed − fitted)\hat{y}_i = m x_i + c \qquad\qquad e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i \quad \text{(observed − fitted)}y^​i​=mxi​+cei​=yi​−y^​i​(observed − fitted)
  • ei>0e_i > 0ei​>0: the point is above the line; the line under-predicts.
  • ei<0e_i < 0ei​<0: the point is below the line; the line over-predicts.
P Code

Which line? polyfit chooses mmm and ccc to make ∑ei2\sum e_i^2∑ei2​, the total squared residual, as small as possible. It is the least-squares idea from Lesson 4.4: there the mean was the best single number; here it is the best straight line. As with the mean, the residuals add up to 0. Unit 6 derives the formulas.

A penguin has residual e=−300e = -300e=−300 g. What does that tell you?

It lies below the line: it is 300 g lighter than the line predicts
Right. e=y−y^<0e = y - \hat{y} < 0e=y−y^​<0 means the observed mass is smaller than the fitted one, so the line over-predicts.
It lies above the line: it is 300 g heavier than the line predicts
The sign is reversed. A residual is observed − fitted, so a negative residual means the penguin is lighter than the line says.
It is 300 g lighter than the average penguin
That would be the distance to the mean, 4,207 g. A residual is the distance to the line, at that penguin’s flipper length.
The line fits badly, because a good line has all residuals 0
Real data scatter, so every line leaves residuals. Least squares makes their squared total as small as possible, not zero.

Choose5c

The penguin line has slope m=50.15m = 50.15m=50.15. What does it tell you?

Penguins with 1 mm longer flippers are about 50 g heavier, on average
Right. The slope is the change in yyy for each step of 1 in xxx: grams per millimetre.
A penguin with 0 mm flippers weighs 50 g
The value at x=0x = 0x=0 is the intercept, ccc, not the slope. (Here it is −5,872 g, which no penguin could weigh: 0 mm is far outside the data.)
Body mass rises by 50% for each extra millimetre
The slope is in the units of yyy per unit of xxx, grams per mm, not a percentage.
Growing a penguin’s flipper by 1 mm makes it 50 g heavier
The line describes how mass and flipper length go together across penguins. It does not say that changing one flipper changes the mass.

Worked example: the residual of the first penguin, by hand

The first penguin has flippers of 181 mm and weighs 3,750 g. Use m=50.15m = 50.15m=50.15 and c=−5872.1c = -5872.1c=−5872.1.

  1. Fitted value: y^=50.15×181−5872.1=9077.15−5872.1≈3205\hat{y} = 50.15 \times 181 - 5872.1 = 9077.15 - 5872.1 \approx 3205y^​=50.15×181−5872.1=9077.15−5872.1≈3205 g.

Lesson check · 5 min

Lesson check

★ essentialSP

Five questions on everything in this lesson.

Choose5c

Which line fits a straight line to arrays x and y and stores its slope in m and intercept in c?

m, c = np.polyfit(x, y, 1)
Right. Degree 1 is a straight line, and polyfit returns the slope first, then the intercept.
c, m = np.polyfit(x, y, 1)
The order is reversed: polyfit returns the highest power first, so the slope comes first. Here c would hold the slope.
m, c = np.polyfit(x, y, 2)
Degree 2 fits a parabola and returns three numbers, so unpacking into two names gives a ValueError.
m, c = np.polyfit(y, x, 1)
That fits xxx against yyy: xxx goes first, then yyy.

A line fitted to exam marks yyy against hours of revision xxx is y^=4.2x+38\hat{y} = 4.2x + 38y^​=4.2x+38. A student revised for 10 hours and scored 71. What is the student’s residual?

That is fitted − observed. A residual is observed − fitted: 71−8071 - 8071−80.
That is the fitted value y^\hat{y}y^​. Subtract it from the observed mark.
You left out the slope: y^=4.2×10+38\hat{y} = 4.2 \times 10 + 38y^​=4.2×10+38, not 38.
First the fitted value, y^=4.2×10+38\hat{y} = 4.2 \times 10 + 38y^​=4.2×10+38.

Choose5c

m and c come from np.polyfit(x, y, 1). Which line computes the residuals?

resid = y - (m * x + c)
Right: observed minus fitted, with the fitted value in brackets.
resid = (m * x + c) - y
That is fitted − observed: every sign is reversed.
resid = y - m * x + c
Without brackets, this subtracts mxmxmx but then adds ccc. Bracket the whole fitted value.
resid = y - y.mean()
That is each value’s distance to the mean. A residual is the distance to the fitted line.

Choose5c

You have x and resid. Which code draws a residual plot?

plt.scatter(x, resid) then plt.axhline(0)
Right: xxx across, residuals up, and a line at 0 to judge the band against.
plt.scatter(resid, x) then plt.axhline(0)
The axes are swapped: xxx goes across, residuals up. The line at 0 is then in the wrong direction too.
plt.scatter(x, y) then plt.plot(x, m * x + c)
That is the data with the fitted line. A residual plot shows what is left over, y−y^y - \hat{y}y−y^​, against xxx.
plt.hist(resid)
A histogram shows how the residuals are spread, but not how they change with xxx, so it cannot show a curve or a fan.
