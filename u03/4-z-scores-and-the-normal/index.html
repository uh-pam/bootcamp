A z-score measures a value in standard deviations from the mean. It puts values on different scales on one common scale.

Worked example · 5 min

Standardising a whole column

★ essentialMSP

The formula works on a whole array at once, like the vector arithmetic in Lesson 2.1.

Worked example: z-scores for all 146 Adelie body masses

The z-scores always have mean 0 and SD 1, as long as the SD is not zero (if every value is the same, you would divide by 0). Why:

  1. The deviations xi−xˉx_i - \bar{x}xi​−xˉ add to 0 (Lesson 2.3), so their mean is 0 — and dividing by sss keeps it 0.
  2. Dividing every value by sss divides the SD by sss too, and s/s=1s / s = 1s/s=1.

This is called standardising.

Use it · 5 min

The bell curve and 68–95–99.7

★ essentialMSP

First, the words for shape. Lesson 2.4 gave you most of them:

WordMeaning
unimodal / bimodalone peak / two peaks (often two groups mixed)
symmetricthe two sides are roughly mirror images
skeweda long tail on one side, named after the tail
bell-shapedunimodal and symmetric, with tails thinning smoothly on both sides

The normal model N(μ,σ)N(\mu, \sigma)N(μ,σ) is the ideal bell: a smooth curve centred at μ\muμ (“mu”) with spread σ\sigmaσ (“sigma”). Greek letters are for the model; xˉ\bar{x}xˉ and sss are for data.

The normal model, three ways
S Picture
M Symbols

The curve’s height at xxx is

f(x)=1σ2π e−12(x−μσ)2f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}\right)^2}f(x)=σ2π​1​e−21​(σx−μ​)2

You will not compute it by hand. Notice the z-score inside it. The 68–95–99.7 rule in z-scores: about 68% of values have ∣z∣<1|z| < 1∣z∣<1, 95% have ∣z∣<2|z| < 2∣z∣<2 and 99.7% have ∣z∣<3|z| < 3∣z∣<3.

Use it · 4 min

Simulating from a model

★ essentialSP

A model is also a recipe for making data. NumPy’s random generator draws values from it:

  • rng.uniform(a, b, size=n): nnn values, equally likely anywhere between aaa and bbb. Its model curve is flat, at height 1/(b−a)1/(b - a)1/(b−a).
  • rng.normal(mu, sigma, size=n): nnn values from N(μ,σ)N(\mu, \sigma)N(μ,σ).
